## The short answer

**Feasible, and most of it already stands.** The beach already has the three coordinates for the real world: earth's map, earth's identity spine, and the clock. Each person also has their own mirror on each one. The clock already reaches back: the Gregorian year is the address, so 1969 is `1969000000`. A read of David's `now` mirror at `1982000000` came back today voiced as "the 1000s … the 1980s … 1982", with nobody's line there yet.

**One thing is new: time that is told after it happened.** The RPG and the earth world are built for the present. Where you stand is one line in your passport, and when you are is only ever stamped. A memory has two times: the time it tells of, and the time it was told. Keeping both needs a ruling, not code (see "The one new thing", below).

## How the RPG does it (checked, not assumed)

The tables (`/w/brackenfoot`, `/w/brackenfoot-open`, and the urb world) put three blocks on one address space:

- **Space**: `spatial:brackenfoot` is the map, nested by scale.
- **Identity**: `identity:brackenfoot` sits at *the same addresses*. "Alone the block is senseless … meaning is the fold of the two, with time appended at the place's own pools." At each place the digits fan into group perspectives (1 the villagers, 2 the crew, 3 the newcomer, 4 the Master). A reader takes the slot their standing selects.
- **People**: `people:brackenfoot` sits at the same addresses too. A principal figure's *states* stand behind its zero, "the same body in time" (`121.202` is the second state of `121.2`).
- **Time**: every place has its room, `pool:<address>` (`pool:211`, `pool:220`). Each act is appended there and stamped by the beach.
- **The character**: `passport:Orik` says who. `history:Orik` is their own journal, and every entry carries who, where and when: `{_: telling, 1: Orik, 2: pool:220:4, 3: 2026-09-29T17:34:47Z, 4: character}`. `witnessed:` and `knows:` hold what is theirs alone.
- **A world's own clock**: `temporal:urb` ("the shape of the now family") carries urb's *history* at past addresses: the eleven-thousands, the Year of Golden Sky, the siege of Bizapul in 11703. Its present days of play sit at the fine rungs. Each hand's own line on it is `temporal:<handle>`. `keeper:urb` 4.7 adds: "histories arrive in VERSIONS … several interpretations of one span side by side."

The sundial states the general law at `pscale://sundial` 8: "Space, time and identity are three blocks on one address space, and meaning lives at the intersection." Each axis has its own verb. Space is **authored**, time is **appended**, and identity is **registered**.

- **The identity spine**, `identity:earth`. These are group perspectives at the map's addresses: Ceidio is held as "home for good", "a year's residence", "a house held from a distance", "the land as work", and so on.
- **David's own mirrors there**:
  - `spatial:happyseaurchin` holds Firbarn Close, the family house.
  - `identity:happyseaurchin` holds how he holds Earth, Europe ("European more than British since both my parents were Portuguese"), the UK, Birmingham and Sutton Coldfield.

- **The passport line**: `Location: *:https://earth.beach.happyseaurchin.com:spatial:earth:31131111110` (the Coach House, Sheffield). It is one line, it lives in the person's own passport, and it is never copied.
- **The read**: `function:earth` 5, "HERE, NOW". It is a read across the three coordinates and never a block: who stands under an address, what each of them is at today, and the place's own record.

**The gap, shown by David's own last year.** Ceidio, then Sutton Coldfield, then Sheffield. The map holds all three places, but the passport holds only the last, and nothing says when he stood where. Each move overwrote the line before it. The earth world keeps places and drops their times. **A history is the moves kept.**

## The one new thing: time told after the fact

The sundial (8.1): "When a handle is is stale the moment it is written — so … the standpoint on time can only ever be stamped." For the present, that is right. For the past it inverts: 1969 does not go stale. A memory is said *now*, about *then*. If it is said **at the address it tells of**, both of its times survive:

- the **address** is when it happened (`1969000000`);
- the beach's **arrival stamp** is when it was told (2026-11-…).

A line whose stamp falls inside its own rung was live. A line whose stamp falls decades later is a memory. That distinction is derived on read and never stored.

**David's own rule agrees** (`stash:happyseaurchin` 17, *tense is position*, 7 September): "its relative positionality (to the presence) indicates it is past — we don't need to encode that into the language — the position holds that." A memory said at `1969000000` reads as the past by its position alone.

**Keel's test in the same entry needs answering:** "if a proposed feature requires writing at a past address, it is the old attractor wearing new clothes." That test guards against **re-tensing**: reaching into a rung that was already voiced, so that it agrees with what happened later. Testimony is different. It voices a rung that was *silent* (the mirror did not exist in 1969), for the first time, and never edits what was said then. `temporal:urb` already writes a world's history at its past addresses on the same grounds. The line to keep sharp is keel's own (point 4): "the address of the edit tells them apart."

**This is decision 1 for David.** (A) A memory is said at the address it tells of, with the stamp as the time of telling. Weft leans to A. (B) Every write is at now, and a memory points back by star-ref. B keeps the present the only place anyone writes, but then "who was here in 1982" needs a derived index instead of the clock itself.

## The shape: no new primitive

1. **Time: the person's own mirror on the clock.** Weft leans to the **`now` mirror itself**, read backwards. On earth it *is* the hand's `temporal:<handle>` (the urb pattern), and "each thing lives once" argues against a second mirror for the same cells. The alternative is a sibling family on the same clock template, if David wants the live `now` kept free of memoir (decision 2).
   - A **move** or an **event** is one line, said at the rung its precision reaches.
   - A **span** is two lines, a start and an end. The state in between is derived on read, as stash 17 says.
   - **Uncertainty** is a coarser rung with the doubt in words ("about 1977–78" at the 1970s). A guessed year is a lie someone else will stand on; a coarser rung is honest.
   - **Detail** nests downward. The decade line is the summary, the year the chapter, the day the scene. A reader walks only as deep as they want.
2. **Space: each line carries the passport's own star-ref**, `*:https://earth.beach.happyseaurchin.com:spatial:earth:<address>`.
   - Places are voiced in the person's `spatial:<handle>`, and the keeper's earth pass (`wake:weft` 9) mints the world's rungs: city, ward, neighbourhood. A house comes only from the person's own words.
   - **A place that is gone keeps its ground as the coordinate.** What stood there, and when, stands as **states** behind the place's zero, in the form `people:brackenfoot` uses for a figure: the hospital, then the ruins, then whatever stands there now.
3. **Identity: who I was there.**
   - `identity:<handle>` at the place's address records how I held it: birthplace, home town, a student's city.
   - The group holds at `identity:earth` are the perspectives: pupils, staff and neighbours at a school.
   - **Registration** is a cohort's collective, settled in landing order (sundial 8: "identity is REGISTERED").
   - **Grains** between people who were there carry the real (`rules:earth` 5: reality is validated socially, never at a gate).
   - The self is never a mirror: the passport's own root already holds David's pivot (1988: Cambridge mathematics declined, social anthropology at Edinburgh).
4. **The read: "HERE, THEN"**, which is `function:earth` 5 generalised from now to any period. At a place address and a period address, it returns every life line whose star-ref walks under the place and whose address walks under the period. It is computed and stored nowhere. At scale it becomes a derived tally like `census:earth`: owned by nobody, rebuilt by the keeper's pass, with names shown only under the same threshold of 24.

## How threads interlock

1. **By coordinate.** Two lines interlock when their place addresses share a prefix and their period addresses overlap: everyone born at Maryfield in the 1960s, the Morgan Academy intake of 1982, Edinburgh's social anthropology students of 1988–92. Sundial 6 says addresses "compare by shared prefix, and the comparison yields orientation rather than distance."
2. **By registration.** A cohort's collective at the place, such as the pupils of one school across one span, makes the claim public and lets people find each other.
3. **By grain.** Two people confirm each other ("we were in the same class"). The grain is also the private channel for memories they don't want public.
4. **What happens when they meet.** The torus-mirror's law says the act on reading is "a reach when two attentions coincide, never a summary." When two life lines meet at a cell, the fold offers a line in each other's parlour, and nothing more.
5. **Accounts stay plural.** Two classmates' tellings of the same year stand side by side, folded at read and never merged into one canon (`identity:brackenfoot`, `keeper:urb` 4.7). The person whose life it is outranks every stored account of it (`rules:earth` 1).

## Resolution, privacy, other people

- **Resolution is address length, in both coordinates.** On the clock, the rung a line stops at *is* its resolution (sundial 3.3). On the map, the digits a person discloses are their grain (`function:earth` 4). Coarse can be public while fine is sealed.
- **Sealing.** A fine rung can be sealed in a colour (a family colour, say) or kept below zero, where `witnessed:` and `knows:` are "theirs alone, never folded into shared layers." Whether a house number from forty years ago is public is the person's call. Someone else lives there now.
- **Other people.**
  - A life names parents, siblings and classmates. The earth law never invents a person's life-facts (`rules:earth` 2).
  - Each person tells their own lines in their own mirror.
  - A suggested default: name people by their role at public rungs, unless they are on the beach and agree. `who-answers` is the policy that applies: the author answers for their own words.

## Scale and cost

- A life mirror can live at the person's own beach. The clock and the star-ref are the same everywhere, so nothing central is needed.
- The map is shared and keeper-minted, with nine digits a rung, taken by arrival. The ladder of city, district and ward keeps each rung's children few, and only places someone stands in are minted. A rung whose nine digits fill mints nothing and waits for a ruling (`function:earth` 7.2). That limit is real for dense places, and far off for now.
- The "here, then" fold is a read. Any index of it is derived and rebuildable, on the `census:earth` pattern.

## What is missing (small)

1. **Decisions 1 and 2** (above): memory at the address it tells, and in which mirror.
2. **One line of law** naming the form: a life line's place is the passport's star-ref; a move is a line; a span is two lines; uncertainty is a coarser rung. It belongs in `function:now` (or the sibling family's law), with a pointer from `function:earth`.
3. **The map:** Dundee and the rungs beneath it, plus the Portuguese town. These come from David's own `spatial:happyseaurchin` and the keeper's pass.
4. **Place states** for places that are gone. This is the `people:brackenfoot` form applied to places. The open question is where they stand: behind the place's zero on `spatial:earth`, or on `identity:earth`.
5. **The "here, then" read**, which is `function:earth` 5 generalised. Later, a page for it: perhaps `/now/<handle>` at a past address, or a life drawn down the clock with its places on the globe.
