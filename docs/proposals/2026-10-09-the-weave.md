## What already stood (nothing below needed inventing)

- **`tree` 4, the inversion.** In the tree convention, authority sits in the social product, not in the base tree. This is David's sentence, written down in July.
- **`tree` 8, the naming.** `spine:V` is the blueprint, `V:<handle>` is each person's mirror, the bare `V` is the fold (the bare name is the authority), and `pool:V` is the room.
- **`tree` 5.1, proposal-by-mirror.** A part the spine lacks is proposed at a new address in your own mirror.
- **`pscale_stream_engage`.** It lists every mirror's reading at an address, with the family's law in the same envelope (V-L-S over a family).
- **`bsp-floor`.** It lays warp, cloth and every mirror side by side at every address in one read.
- **The `experiences` fold.** It was "compiled by the steward until a beach-crab does it", and it was compiled once in July and never again. Readings kept landing (Farlane, Ayush, check-hand, happyhedgehog, David), and nothing re-folded them into the page.
- **The April world-compressor** (`scripts/world-compressor.ts` in this repo). It folded authors' observations into a world's canon on a clock: the same mechanism with another face.

The gap was one thing: **nobody kept the fold**. Readings landed in mirrors, the page went on rendering a block that only its author could change, and the loop closed through David's attention.

## The architecture

```
                     spine:<name>   the WARP: the parts, each saying only what it is for
                          │          (written once by the framer; parts appended, never renumbered)
                          │
  reader's box ──▶  <name>:<handle>  each reader's WEFT: their own mirror, a reading at the part it
  (the page, or         │           answers, latched to their key on first use; revisable only by them
   any assistant)       │
                        ▼
              ┌───── the KEEPER'S PASS (function:weave 4) ─────┐
              │ NOTICE   roster + one bsp-floor; rest if nothing new
              │ WEAVE    each part with something new, in the page's voice
              │ ARCHIVE  archive:<name>:<date>, then the cloth whole
              │ REPORT   one line at pool:<name>: what changed, whose readings
              └────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
                     <name>         the CLOTH: the page as it now reads, latched to the keeper;
                          │          its 9 is the weave record (when, by whom, from whose readings)
                          ▼
              happyseaurchin.com/portal   renders the cloth part by part; every reading
                                          beside its part; a box at each part
```

**The law** is `function:weave`, the form. Each family adopts it with a small law of its own (`function:portal`), which holds three dials:
- `1 mode: weave`
- `4 keeper: weft`
- `5 consent: open`

The other forms on the beach work the same way. `function:document` with `function:autumn-revenue` is the pattern followed. The form's branches are:
- 2: the reader
- 3: a reading
- 4: the pass, a spindle down 4.1 → 4.1111, with side-rooms
- 5: consent
- 6: cadence
- 7: the loom (the woven families, so a routine's work is held on the beach and not only in its prompt)
- 8: founding
- 9: provenance

**Consent is a dial, not a second mechanism.** With `consent: open` (the portal), the pass writes the cloth and the page changes. With `consent: held <handle>` (a welcome, say), the pass weaves into the keeper's own tree, `tree:<name>:<keeper>`, and the page shows the proposal a tap away. The holder answers with a reading at 9 of their own mirror that opens CONSENT or OBJECT (token-first, as tree 9.3 counts readings). Consent is a word on the cloth and never a gate on the readings: anyone can still read and be heard.

**The routine is thin.** "Weft · weaver", twice a day (07:49 and 19:49 London), keyed the same way as the earth keeper:
1. Read `function:weave` whole.
2. Take the families the loom names weft keeper of.
3. Run the pass for each.
4. Log one `daily:weft` line.

A pass with nothing new rests after two reads.

**Who pays.** The keeper pays: a few reads twice a day, and a short synthesis when someone has spoken. Any hand can keep a woven family. A page kept by another keeper names that keeper at its law's 4, and the loom opens when a second keeper arrives.

## Decisions, and why

- **The cloth is latched to the keeper rather than left open.** An open fold that a page renders to strangers can be defaced between passes. A latched cloth is written only by the keeper, and the keeper writes only what readings carry. Authority stays in the collective, by transparency rather than permission:
  - every reading stands beside its part in its reader's own name;
  - every cloth that ever stood is in the archive;
  - the weave record at 9 says what was done with each reading;
  - anyone can weave their own (`tree:<name>:<handle>`) and offer it.

  A keeper whose cloth stops answering its readings is replaced at the family's law.
- **Two ways of knowing what is new, one per reader.**
  - The page uses the index's `touched` stamps: a mirror that moved after the cloth is "waiting for the next weave". That is block-level, honest and free.
  - The keeper uses the cloth's record at `9.<part>`, which matches readings by reader and first words. That is exact, at any depth, and needs no stamp the bsp tool does not show.
- **The loom is a catalogue, not a register.** A family is woven because its own law says `mode: weave`. The loom lists it so the routine finds it in one read instead of scanning every law.
- **No new primitive and no new tool.** Every part of this is blocks, the two read instruments (`pscale_stream_engage`, `bsp-floor`), and an o-page. A reading is a stream `say`, so any assistant can leave one without the page.

## Risks, named

- **Readings are untrusted text read by a keyed mind.** The routine's prompt and the law both say readings are data, and a reading that would make the page worse for a stranger is not woven, with the reason recorded at 9. The archive makes any bad weave one reading away from repair. This is the same exposure the earth keeper already carries; it is worth watching on the first busy day.
- **A keeper's taste is a single point.** The keeper's taste is checkable, not hidden (the record, the archive, the readings beside every part). Rival weaves can stand at `tree:<name>:<handle>`.
- **Stale truths.** The first cloth's "what is still rough" was drawn from July and August readings, and some of it may already be fixed. That is exactly what a reading corrects.
